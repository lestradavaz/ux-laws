# Serial Position Effect

## Principle and evidence

Position in a sequence can affect what people later recall. Organize sequences deliberately while keeping important information accessible without depending on memory.

The catalog summarizes primacy and recency effects from memory research. Translating list recall into navigation placement is a heuristic. Visible menus, practiced commands, dynamic lists, and delayed recall are different tasks; position alone does not establish findability.

## Observable triggers

- Instructions bury a consequential condition within a long sequence.
- Users remember initial setup and the final action but miss intermediate prerequisites.
- A proposal moves commands to the ends solely to improve recall.

## Implementation actions

1. Identify whether the task requires remembering a sequence, scanning visible options, or following a learned workflow. Use ordering suited to that task rather than treating all lists alike.
2. Lead instructions with purpose and prerequisites; conclude with a clear result or verification action. Keep critical constraints visible at the decision they affect even if they appeared earlier.
3. Use meaningful and stable ordering such as chronology, task sequence, alphabetic lookup, or frequency when justified. Preserve familiar command positions unless evidence warrants a change.
4. Provide persistent summaries, checklists, or searchable reference for necessary details. Do not make an important middle step depend on unaided recall.
5. Test interpretation and execution separately from later recollection. Check RTL, translated content, and responsive arrangements because reading sequence may change.

## Example

A deployment checklist begins with destination and permissions, follows the actual validation sequence, and ends with a verified result. A persistent summary shows destination beside the final Publish action, so remembering the first instruction is not the only safeguard. Errors link back to the exact incomplete step.

## Counterexample

An administrator moves Delete account to the last position to make it memorable, violating an established menu convention and placing a dangerous action beside the most-used command without additional distinction.

## Limits and conflicts

Beginning and end are not universally the best locations for controls. Platform conventions, motor travel, urgency, and logical sequence can matter more. Recall does not equal successful action. Never hide material costs, warnings, or refusal routes in the middle because they might receive less attention. Use this principle to support understanding, not exploit weak recall.

## Acceptance checks

- Ordering has a task-relevant rationale beyond expected memorability.
- Critical details are visible when their consequences matter.
- Reading, focus, and translated sequence remain coherent.
- Users execute all necessary steps accurately, including middle steps, without memory-dependent safeguards.
