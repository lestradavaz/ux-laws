# Flow

## Principle and evidence

Focused work benefits from clear goals, useful feedback, control, and a challenge suited to the person's skill. Support continuity without preventing pauses or exit.

Flow is a psychological concept describing absorbed activity. It is not a promise that removing interruptions creates that state, nor a justification to maximize time spent. Evaluate whether people accomplish their intended work effectively and can regulate their engagement.

## Observable triggers

- An editor interrupts repeated actions with unnecessary confirmations or tips.
- Experts cannot use shortcuts, while novices receive little contextual support.
- Background updates steal focus, move content, or reset working state.

## Implementation actions

1. Identify the repeated task loop and its natural stopping points. Keep controls, feedback, selection, and spatial context stable within that loop.
2. Offer progressive learning: clear primary actions for new users, discoverable shortcuts and batch operations for experienced users. Keep the same result and recovery model across paths.
3. Classify interruptions by urgency and consequence. Defer noncritical tips; expose critical status clearly with the least disruptive presentation that still supports safe action.
4. Maintain responsive local interaction and preserve drafts, selection, scroll position, and undo history according to product expectations. Handle asynchronous changes without overwriting active work.
5. Make pausing, saving, resuming, and leaving practical. Offer meaningful completion and optional reminders rather than endless tasks, streak pressure, or reward loops that displace the user's goal.

## Example

An editor lets a user adjust a slide, preview it, and undo changes without opening confirmation dialogs. Export status stays in a persistent panel while editing continues. A collaboration conflict names the affected slide and offers a comparison instead of silently replacing work. Shortcut help is available when requested, and the draft resumes at the same slide after restart.

## Counterexample

A productivity app labels forced daily streaks and autoplay tutorials as flow. It blocks exit with guilt messaging and interrupts users who already understand the current tool.

## Limits and conflicts

Flow must yield to safety-critical interruption, informed consent, or prevention of major irreversible loss. Reducing friction can also remove valuable checkpoints. People differ in ability and experience, so flexible support is safer than silently increasing difficulty. Do not infer a psychological state from engagement duration alone.

## Acceptance checks

- Representative repeated actions preserve selection, focus, and context.
- Important failures are noticed while noncritical updates do not interrupt the loop.
- Novice and expert paths both support completion and recovery.
- Users can pause, leave, and resume without disproportionate loss or pressure.
