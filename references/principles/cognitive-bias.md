# Cognitive Bias

## Principle and evidence

Judgment can follow shortcuts that systematically distort interpretation. Evaluate both the choices an interface presents and the assumptions shaping its design.

Cognitive bias is a family of phenomena, not a single mechanism with one remedy. The catalog uses confirmation bias as an illustration. Naming a bias does not prove it caused a result, and awareness alone does not remove its effects.

## Observable triggers

- A team selects only feedback that supports a preferred redesign.
- A default, ordering, price anchor, or framing changes a consequential decision.
- An experiment optimizes selection or retention while excluding regret, errors, or user outcomes.

## Implementation actions

1. Write a falsifiable prediction before evaluation: what behavior should improve and what result would challenge the proposal? Include competing explanations such as unclear wording or missing information.
2. Review defaults, comparison order, framing, and recommendations for their consequences. Make the effect of a selection explicit before commitment.
3. Present costs, benefits, risks, and alternatives on comparable terms. Use clear denominators, time periods, and units for quantitative claims.
4. Recruit relevant audiences rather than convenient supporters alone. Separate observations, interpretation, and confidence when reporting results.
5. Protect choice and reversal. Avoid fake scarcity, disguised promotions, preselected consequential consent, and interfaces that make refusal disproportionately difficult.

## Example

A subscription comparison labels one option Recommended. The team records that its basis is collaboration features for multi-person teams, not popularity. The same screen shows the monthly billing basis and cancellation terms for every plan. Evaluation checks whether people choose a plan suited to their needs and understand its cost, rather than counting upgrades alone.

## Counterexample

A default annual plan shows a small monthly equivalent while the alternative shows its full monthly price. More annual purchases are called better UX without checking whether users understood the upfront charge.

## Limits and conflicts

Different biases can interact, and many observed effects have contextual boundaries. Treat proposed explanations as hypotheses. Safety-critical or financial choices require domain validation beyond this card. Convenience defaults can help when informed, reversible, and audience appropriate; a bias label is not an automatic prohibition.

## Acceptance checks

- The decision rationale includes disconfirming evidence and an explicit uncertainty statement.
- Consequential choices show comparable material facts before confirmation.
- Declining and reversing remain practical for the audience.
- Evaluation includes understanding and adverse outcomes, not only business conversion.
